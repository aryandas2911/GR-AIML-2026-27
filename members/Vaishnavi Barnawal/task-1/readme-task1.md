# Freshness Forecast

## 1. Project Overview
Freshness Forecast is an AI/ML project proposal that aims to predict how many days fruits and vegetables may remain fresh. It is designed to help households, students, small vendors, and canteens reduce food waste and manage produce better.

2. Problem Statement
It is difficult to estimate the remaining freshness of fruits and vegetables just by looking at them. Existing solutions may identify whether produce is fresh or spoiled, but they may not tell users how many days are left or what action they should take.

3. Proposed Solution
The proposed system allows users to:
- Take a photo of a fruit or vegetable.
- Select its storage location, such as a refrigerator or counter.
- Identify the type of produce and its decay stage.
- Estimate the remaining days of freshness.
- Receive storage suggestions, use-first alerts, and recipe ideas.

4. Role of AI/ML
The project proposes using:
- Computer Vision to analyse images of fruits and vegetables.
- Classification to identify produce and its freshness or decay stage.
- Regression to estimate the number of days remaining.

The prediction may also consider storage conditions and local weather.

5. Data Requirements
The proposed system may use fresh and spoiled produce images, daily time-lapse photographs, storage conditions, shelf-life references, local weather information, and user feedback.

6. Challenges and Limitations
- Limited datasets containing the actual number of days remaining before spoilage.
- Changes in lighting, backgrounds, and produce varieties may affect predictions.
- Internal spoilage may not be visible in photographs.
- The system needs to protect user privacy and work efficiently on budget smartphones.

7. Expected Impact
Freshness Forecast aims to help people plan meals, store produce appropriately, reduce avoidable food waste, and make better use of groceries.

8. Current Status
This submission presents the project idea, proposed workflow, AI/ML approach, required data, existing related solutions, and potential challenges. The presentation describes a proposal; it does not claim that a working prediction model has already been built.
